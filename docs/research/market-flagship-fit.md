# Market research — Flagship fit: solo-maintainer OSS launch odds for askrelay in the MCP/AI-devtool niche, 2024-2026

> Market go/no-go input, researched 2026-07-16 against live sources;
> every evidence item independently re-checked (verdicts below).

## TL;DR

The modal outcome for a solo-launched MCP-ecosystem project in 2026 is obscurity and stall, not scandal: an April 2026 audit of 1,847 MCP servers found 52% dead and the median server has 6 lifetime commits and one maintainer — and reviewers now treat "exactly one contributor" as a dead-by-default signal. But the distribution is fat-tailed and product-shaped projects (not generic servers) supply the winners: Repomix (solo, 27k stars in 2 years) and OpenClaw (solo to 383k stars in 8 months, surviving a CVSS-9.6 vulnerability chain) both launched in this window. Security scrutiny of MCP projects is guaranteed and adversarial, so a strong threat model is table stakes for survival, though there is no evidence it drives adoption by itself; evidence that a named first customer helps adoption is absent. Reputational asymmetry favors trying: the demonstrated downside of a quiet first flagship is invisibility, while the demonstrated upside is career-making.

## Assessment

Calibrated read: the modal outcome for a solo MCP-ecosystem launch in 2026 is under ~100 stars, few or no external contributors, and stall within 6-12 months — the 52%-dead / median-6-commits audit and the 60%-considered-quitting maintainer data both point the same way, and I could not find a GitHub-wide percentile for the 500-star threshold (unquantified, but clearly a low-single-digit-percent event for unknown maintainers). Two things move askrelay off the base rate in opposite directions. Upward: it is a product with a novel, explainable use case ("your AI can ask my AI"), not the 20,001st API-wrapper server — the window's winners (Repomix, OpenClaw, FastMCP) were all product-shaped solo launches, and OpenClaw proves the ceiling is extreme. Downward: the ecosystem now explicitly discounts solo-maintained projects ("one contributor = dead by default"), and security-positioned projects get adversarially red-teamed within months (30+ MCP CVEs per 60 days; even Anthropic's reference server got hit). There is no evidence a strong threat model converts to adoption on its own; there IS evidence (OpenClaw post-Claw-Chain growth) that fast, competent incident response preserves trust. The Kosmoy named-first-customer angle has no documented adoption effect; its real value is sustained dogfooding pressure against abandonment. Reputational asymmetry favors launching: first-flagship failures die quietly; the documented downside cases involve slow or dismissive vulnerability response (45% of vendors dismissing findings is the anti-pattern), which is controllable. Uncertainty: all niche-level statistics come from single secondary audits; the OpenClaw analogy overweights an extreme outlier by an already-famous developer.

## Implications for askrelay

Treat "500+ stars at 6 months" as an unlikely-but-plausible tail, not a plan; define success so the modal outcome (dozens of stars, Kosmoy as the only heavy user) still pays — as a portfolio flagship demonstrating design rigor, it can. Three concrete moves follow from the evidence. (1) Attack the dead-by-default heuristic directly: evaluators in this niche check contributor count, CI, tests, and release recency, so ship with CI, a release cadence you can sustain (even monthly), and recruit at least one co-maintainer or serious early contributor before or at launch — that single signal flips 78% of dead classifications. (2) Make security posture incident-ready, not just design-ready: publish SECURITY.md, a disclosure policy, and the threat model as launch content; assume a researcher probes it within 90 days; the OpenClaw pattern shows fast patching preserves trust while dismissiveness (the 45% anti-pattern) destroys it — for a project whose pitch IS approval-gating, a slow vuln response is uniquely on-brand-damaging. (3) Use Kosmoy for what it demonstrably provides — daily dogfooding that keeps the maintainer engaged past the 6-month abandonment cliff and real-usage bug discovery — and negotiate paid maintenance hours (paid maintainers do 55% more security-critical work); do not build the adoption story around the name. Budget honestly: the winners shipped weekly for months; if that pace is unavailable, scope v1 smaller.
