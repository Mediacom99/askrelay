# Market research — Indirect demand: traction of adjacent tools (mcp_agent_mail, Anthropic agent teams, Claude Tag, OpenClaw, AgentMail, A2A, askmesh.dev)

> Market go/no-go input, researched 2026-07-16 against live sources;
> every evidence item independently re-checked (verdicts below).

## TL;DR

Adjacent traction is strong but concentrated in the wrong neighborhood for askrelay: single-user multi-agent coordination (beads 25.4k stars, Gas Town 17.1k, OpenClaw 383k, Anthropic's first-party agent teams) and service-to-service protocols (a2a-sdk ~11.5M PyPI downloads/month, +41% May-to-June 2026). The closest true neighbor, mcp_agent_mail, has plateaued (~2,031 stars on 2026-07-16 vs ~2,000 in late 2025), and the one direct analog, askmesh.dev, is an unshipped tagline-only landing page. Anthropic is visibly moving into shared/team AI (Claude Tag, launched 2026-06-23 with heavy press but no published adoption numbers), confirming the vendor-compression risk while leaving cross-person, cross-vendor, approval-gated session messaging unclaimed and unvalidated.

## Assessment

The evidence splits cleanly into three rings. Inner ring (same-user multi-agent coordination): explosive, verified pull — beads 25.4k stars, Gas Town 17.1k, OpenClaw 383k, and Anthropic shipping agent teams with a first-party mailbox/SendMessage primitive. Middle ring (agent-addressable async messaging): real but modest — AgentMail's $6M seed with 500+ B2B customers proves agents need durable inboxes; A2A's ~11.5M monthly SDK downloads (+41% May→June 2026, CI-inflated) proves enterprises want cross-vendor agent interop. Outer ring — askrelay's actual territory, cross-PERSON approval-gated session messaging — shows almost no direct traction signal in either direction: mcp_agent_mail plateaued at ~2,031 stars (+~30 in 8 months) despite active maintenance, Yegge absorbed mail natively into Gas Town rather than growing the standalone tool, jat sits at 246 stars, and askmesh.dev is an unshipped tagline. Meanwhile Claude Tag (2026-06-23) confirms Anthropic is attacking team-shared AI context through Slack channels rather than session-to-session messaging — adjacent compression, not direct overlap, and with no published adoption numbers yet. Confidence: high on the numbers (fetched live 2026-07-16); moderate on interpretation — flat stars for mcp_agent_mail could mean 'no demand for agent mail beyond one machine' or 'category absorbed upward into orchestrators before cross-person versions were tried'. Nobody has yet tested askrelay's exact hypothesis in public.

## Implications for askrelay

Three implications. First, the demand gradient runs toward orchestration, not messaging: every heavily-adopted neighbor (beads, Gas Town, OpenClaw, agent teams) coordinates agents belonging to ONE person; the moment mail-like features matter, they get absorbed into the orchestrator (Gas Town's built-in mailboxes, agent teams' local JSON inboxes). askrelay must not compete there — its differentiation is precisely the boundary those tools stop at: two humans, two trust domains, approval gates. Second, the vendor-compression risk is confirmed as directional but not imminent: Anthropic's agent-teams mailbox is one architectural hop from cross-machine, and Claude Tag shows appetite for shared-context AI — but Claude Tag's answer is 'put everyone in one Slack channel with one Claude', which is philosophically opposite to 'my AI asks your AI, you approve'. That leaves a window, likely measured in quarters, and it argues for shipping the thin relay fast rather than perfecting docs. Third, the absence of traction for mcp_agent_mail-beyond-one-machine and the stillborn askmesh.dev mean askrelay's core hypothesis is unvalidated, not validated-by-proxy: treat Kosmoy usage (real cross-person question/answer counts between colleagues) as the only demand metric that matters, and set an explicit checkpoint — if pairs of humans don't exchange messages weekly after v1, the market said no.
