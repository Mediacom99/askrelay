# Commercialization research — Hosted-tier economics of self-hostable OSS (comparable cases, real numbers)

> Input to the commercialization-posture decision (D-13/D-08 review), researched
> 2026-07-19 against live sources; every evidence item independently re-checked.

## TL;DR

Paid hosted tiers on self-hostable OSS follow a brutal power law: winners (Supabase $170M ARR, n8n $40M, PostHog $57M) had VC, teams, and proven categories; the best bootstrapped case (Plausible) still took ~11 months to reach $400 MRR and 3 years to $1M ARR in a high-demand category. Solo-run hosted tiers top out at hobby-to-modest income on multi-year horizons (Healthchecks.io: $14k MRR after 9 years; Miniflux: $15/yr side project), and the closest domain comparable, Zulip, needed founder subsidy plus a 2026 nonprofit pivot after a decade. Conversion of free/self-host users to paid runs well below 1%, so at askrelay's modal outcome (dozens of stars, one team) a paid tier earns approximately zero while adding billing/support/uptime ops. Timing finding: Apache-2.0 + DCO does NOT block a later open-core/proprietary pivot on future versions (Apache grants sublicense + derivative-work rights); what locks irreversibly at first external contribution is exclusivity — published code stays Apache for everyone forever — whereas the documented AGPL-relay fallback is the option that would genuinely hard-lock.

## Assessment

The honest base rate at askrelay's modal outcome is unambiguous: a paid hosted tier earns roughly €0–200/month. Every project with material hosting revenue had, before that revenue, either tens of thousands of active users in an already-monetized category (analytics, scheduling, automation, backends) or years of subsidized grind — usually both. Plausible, the strongest bootstrapped analog, needed 11 months to reach $400 MRR with strong category demand askrelay does not have; Zulip, the closest domain comparable (messaging, cloud + self-host, strong OSS ethics), never made subscription revenue fully carry the team in ten years and just became a grant-seeking nonprofit. Solo operators who succeeded either kept hosting as a slow-burn lifestyle business (Healthchecks: 6.5 years to sole-income) or skipped hosting entirely for paid licensing atop a huge pre-existing install base (Sidekiq). Failures confirm no floor: Papercups had YC money and stars and still shut its cloud. Conversion of free users to paid is well under 1%, so 'dozens of stars, one team' predicts zero paying customers. Switching posture to open-core or proprietary now would therefore change nothing about near-term revenue while damaging the flagship-OSS and trust goals of a consent/security product — and Cal.com's 2026 closed-source pivot shows the reputational cost of closing later is real but survivable only with an established paying base. The maintainer's prior (Apache-2.0 + free hosted demo, paid-tier-ready hardening) is the evidence-consistent posture; the paid tier is an option to exercise after traction, not a revenue plan.

## Implications for askrelay

Keep D-13 and D-08. The decisive new finding is about TIMING and its direction: the premise that DCO contributions lock askrelay's licensing is inverted for a permissive license. Apache-2.0 grants sublicense and derivative-work rights, so even after external DCO contributions the maintainer can lawfully take future versions open-core, BUSL, or fully proprietary (Cal.com-style) with only notice-preservation duties — no CLA needed. What locks irrevocably at the first external contribution (indeed at first publication) is exclusivity: everything already shipped stays Apache for everyone, so any later closed pivot competes against its own free fork and any cloud vendor may host askrelay. By contrast, the documented fallback — AGPL for the relay — is the one that genuinely hard-locks: once AGPL code contains external DCO contributions, a proprietary or hosted-exclusive pivot requires rewriting or hunting consent. So Apache+DCO is the maximally option-preserving posture, and the fallback should be reframed as 'exercise before meaningful external contributions or not at all.' Practically: build the hosted-hardening work package paid-tier-ready (multi-tenancy boundaries, quotas, billing hooks stubbed) but do not stand up billing — at dozens of stars it would buy ops burden for zero revenue; Zulip and Healthchecks show even good products need years. First-dollar path at this scale is employer sponsorship (Kosmoy), GitHub Sponsors, or Sidekiq-style paid add-ons later — not hosting invoices. Revisit paid hosting only if the D-08 free instance shows sustained multi-team organic usage (a pre-registerable trigger, e.g., 25+ active external orgs).

## Open questions

- Real paid-vs-self-host conversion for the closest comparables is unpublished: Plausible/Cal.com never released what fraction of self-hosters converted to cloud — the <1% figure is category-level, not product-level.
- Papercups' revenue at shutdown is unverified from a primary source; a founder post-mortem would sharpen the failure base rate for YC-grade open-core chat tools.
- Does the free hosted instance (D-08) create demand evidence a paid tier could measure (active external orgs, retention), and what threshold should pre-register the 'stand up billing' decision?
- Would Kosmoy sponsorship or a Sidekiq-style paid daemon/enterprise-feature license outperform hosting economically at small scale, given hosting's fixed ops cost and askrelay's per-org (not per-seat) value shape?
- If the AGPL-relay fallback is ever exercised, exactly which pre-fork external contributions exist and are they small enough to rewrite — i.e., when does the fallback's window actually close?
- Does Anthropic/OpenAI ToS clarification on unattended auto-reply (deferred to v1.1) change willingness to pay enough to alter the hosted-tier calculus?
