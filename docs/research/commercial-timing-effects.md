# Commercialization research — Paid tier at launch vs added later: adoption effects (sequencing evidence for askrelay's D-08/D-13 posture)

> Input to the commercialization-posture decision (D-13/D-08 review), researched
> 2026-07-19 against live sources; every evidence item independently re-checked.

## TL;DR

Across every case verified live, charging from day one did not measurably slow OSS adoption: Plausible charged four months before it even open-sourced and hit $1M ARR; n8n carried commercial restrictions from its first release and is the most-adopted project in the sample (197k stars). The documented failures run the other way — Babel and core-js stayed free and ran out of money, and ntfy's free hosted relay converted 25k daily users into 20 paying subscribers while its paid launch killed donations. Community anger attaches to dishonest labeling and retroactive removal (n8n's "gaslighting" threads, Cal.com's April 2026 closed-source pivot), not to the existence of a paid tier. Under Apache-2.0+DCO, the first external contribution locks only the free direction (code stays forkable forever) — adding a paid hosted tier later is never blocked; only the AGPL-relay fallback must be decided before contributions arrive.

## Evidence

1. 🟢 **[primary, verified 2026-07-19 (post covers 2019-2022)]** Plausible launched paid subscriptions in May 2019 ($64 MRR) BEFORE open-sourcing the code in Sept 2019 (MIT, later AGPL Oct 2020); reached $1M ARR by June 2022. Donations raised $30 in six months vs $8,500+ MRR from the paid cloud at the same time — paid-from-day-one funded the project; donations were noise.
   - Source: <https://plausible.io/blog/open-source-saas>
2. 🟢 **[primary, 2020-10-04, verified 2026-07-19]** Plausible's Oct 2020 Show HN: no meaningful objection to the paid cloud tier; commenters praised the open license and business-model transparency. The only monetization concern raised was competitors reselling the code (a threat TO the maintainer, not from them) — no 'they'll paywall it' suspicion visible at launch.
   - Source: <https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=24696145>
3. 🟢 **[primary, 2024-02, verified 2026-07-19]** Plausible Community Edition (Feb 2024): self-hosted version renamed, some newer features made cloud-only, trademarks registered. HN reaction was mild concern, not a firestorm — a 5-year-old honest paid-tier posture absorbed a mild enclosure move without trust collapse.
   - Source: <https://plausible.io/blog/community-edition>
4. 🟢 **[primary, 2023-07-31 announcement; stats 2026-07-19]** Umami is the added-later control case: MIT OSS from July 2020, Umami Cloud beta (free) announced July 31 2023, paid pricing after beta, simplified to $20/mo (Sept 2024) with an explicit 'self-hosting is free forever' pledge. Result today: 37,735 stars, ~375 contributors (GitHub API, 2026-07-19) — healthy, but no evidence it adopted faster than paid-from-day-one peers; n8n and Cal.com outgrew it.
   - Source: <https://github.com/umami-software/umami/discussions/1672>
5. 🟢 **[primary, 2026-07-19]** n8n has been commercially restricted since first release (Apache-2.0 + Commons Clause in 2019, Sustainable Use License from 2022 — never OSI open source) yet shows 197,000 stars and ~427 contributors (GitHub API, 2026-07-19) — the largest adoption in this sample. Restrictions from day one did not deter users or contributors at scale.
   - Source: <https://github.com/n8n-io/n8n>
6. 🟡 **[secondary, 2022-09, verified 2026-07-19]** n8n's community friction was entirely about LABELING, not charging: HN threads titled 'N8n is not open source and your project is gaslighting its users' (2022) attacked the open-source claim under a restricted license. The lesson is that honesty about terms disarms; the paid tier itself was never the grievance.
   - Source: <https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=32797853>
7. 🟡 **[secondary, 2021, verified 2026-07-19]** Cal.com launched open-core with a paid hosted plan from the start (2021, AGPL core + commercial /ee): #1 Product of the Day, Week, and Month on Product Hunt (April 2021), trending on HN/GitHub — the paid tier at launch coexisted with explosive adoption.
   - Source: <https://www.producthunt.com/stories/how-this-open-source-calendly-alternative-rocketed-to-product-of-the-day>
8. 🟢 **[primary, 2026-04-15, verified 2026-07-19]** Cal.com then closed its commercial codebase in April 2026, leaving a stripped MIT 'Cal.diy' community edition (no Teams, SSO, Workflows, Insights), citing AI-assisted vulnerability discovery; tech press reaction was skeptical ('AI... or the perfect scapegoat'). The 'they will paywall it eventually' fear is rational and has a fresh, high-profile precedent — enabled by the company owning/relicensing rights over its repo, exactly what askrelay's no-CLA DCO posture structurally prevents.
   - Source: <https://cal.com/blog/cal-diy-open-source-to-closed-source>
9. 🟡 **[secondary, 2023-06, verified 2026-07-19]** ntfy (open-source push relay — the closest shape to askrelay's hosted instance): maintainer reported ~25,000 daily active users on the free hosted tier vs 20 paying subscribers (June 2023), an overly generous free tier (originally 17k msgs/day, cut to 250), fail2ban needed against abuse, and — critically — 'ever since I started offering paid subscriptions, donations have reduced and new donations have pretty much stopped.'
   - Source: <https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=36173584>
10. 🟢 **[primary, 2021-05-10, verified 2026-07-19]** Reverse failure, stayed free too long: Babel announced May 2021 it was 'running out of money' — needed ~$333k/yr for paid maintainers, roughly 2x incoming donations, despite Airbnb/Facebook/Salesforce sponsorship and millions of users.
   - Source: <https://babeljs.io/blog/2021/05/10/funding-update>
11. 🟡 **[secondary, 2023-02, verified 2026-07-19]** Reverse failure, no paid surface at all: core-js (9B+ downloads, >50% of top websites) yielded its sole maintainer ~$57/month from README funding mentions; adding a postinstall Patreon message produced a 'continuous stream of hate.' Free-forever with donation-only funding is the documented death path for solo maintainers.
   - Source: <https://www.thestack.technology/core-js-maintainer-denis-pusharev-license-broke-angry/>
12. 🟢 **[primary, 2021-04/05, verified 2026-07-19]** MinIO relicensed Apache-2.0 → AGPLv3 in April-May 2021 WITHOUT a CLA: legally executed by keeping prior third-party contributions in Apache-licensed '-contrib' files, but the silent change triggered a contributor-consent controversy and lasting reputational damage ('open source darling to cautionary tale'). Precedent: permissive + DCO does not legally block a license change, but makes it messy and trust-expensive.
   - Source: <https://github.com/minio/minio/discussions/12157>
13. 🟢 **[primary, 2024-03 (Immich), 2024-09 (Umami tweet), verified 2026-07-19]** What disarms paywall suspicion — explicit pledges plus structure: Umami's 'self-hosting is free forever' (official account, Sept 2024); FUTO/Immich's 'never remove features, never paywall functionality' pledge alongside optional paid licenses, which earned 5x Immich's lifetime donations within one month (their own figure). A DCO-without-CLA is the structural version: analyses confirm a DCO grants the maintainer no relicensing rights, unlike the CLAs that enabled the Cal.com/HashiCorp-style moves.
   - Source: <https://immich.app/blog/immich-joins-futo>

## Assessment

The sequencing question has a clear answer: no verified case shows an early paid tier slowing OSS adoption. Plausible charged before open-sourcing and its Show HN was warm; Cal.com launched open-core-with-paid and swept Product Hunt; n8n was commercially restricted from day one and out-adopted everything here. The added-later case (Umami) did fine but shows no adoption premium for waiting. Community hostility, where it occurred, was triggered by (a) calling restricted software "open source" (n8n) and (b) retroactively enclosing what was free (Cal.com 2026, MinIO 2021) — never by honestly charging for hosting from the start. Meanwhile the failure mode with the most bodies is staying free too long: Babel, core-js, and ntfy's 25k-users/20-payers hosted tier, which also shows that launching a paid tier late *cannibalizes donations without converting users* once a generous free tier has set expectations. On timing/locking: under Apache-2.0 + DCO, the first external contribution locks only the *free* direction — published code stays forkable forever, and a later restrictive relicense becomes the messy MinIO path — but it never blocks adding a paid hosted tier or proprietary add-ons, because Apache-2.0 permits proprietary derivatives. The only decision that must precede first external contribution is whether to exercise the AGPL-relay fallback; open-core-later stays open indefinitely. Caveat: all comparators had 10k+ stars; there is no data on paid-tier effects at dozens-of-stars scale, where the tier is moot anyway.

## Implications for askrelay

The maintainer's prior survives, with two sharpenings. (1) Keep D-13 exactly as is — Apache-2.0 + DCO + no-CLA is not just compatible with a future paid hosted tier, it is the strongest available anti-rug-pull signal in a month when Cal.com's closure made the suspicion front-page rational. Advertise it as such in the README: "no CLA means we structurally cannot pull a Cal.com." (2) Revise D-08's framing, not its substance: announce at launch, in writing, that self-hosting is free forever and that the hosted instance is a quota-limited demo that MAY become a paid tier — the evidence says stating paid intent early costs nothing (Plausible), while springing it on an entrenched free user base later converts almost nobody and kills donations (ntfy). Give the free instance hard quotas from day one (ntfy's 17k-to-250 msgs/day retreat is the cautionary tale for a relay-shaped service) and build the hosted-hardening work package paid-ready as planned. (3) Timing: the Apache-vs-AGPL-relay fork in the road is the ONLY thing that closes at first external DCO contribution; open-core, paid hosting, and proprietary add-ons remain open forever. Closed-source remains correctly rejected — for a consent/security product, Cal.com's "AI security" rationale drew immediate skepticism even with five years of goodwill.

## Open questions

- Is the AGPL-relay fallback formally abandoned, or does it need a dated decision? It cannot be cleanly exercised after the first external DCO contribution (MinIO precedent shows the messy path), so it should be resolved in the architecture doc before code lands.
- What are realistic infra + abuse-moderation costs for a free message relay at small scale? ntfy documents the conversion problem but published no cost figures; a relay carrying human-approved messages (not push spam) may have a very different abuse profile — no documented small-relay case was found.
- Conversion base rates at sub-1k-star scale: every comparator here had 10k+ stars before monetizing; there is no evidence on whether a paid tier matters at all at askrelay's modal outcome (dozens of stars, employer as only heavy user) — which argues for paid-READY, not paid-at-launch engineering effort.
- Does the natural paid feature (unattended auto-reply, deferred to v1.1 pending Anthropic ToS clarification) survive vendor ToS review? If the flagship paid feature is ToS-blocked, the paid tier reduces to managed hosting alone, weakening the open-core case.
- If Kosmoy becomes founding sponsor AND the repo later moves to a neutral org (D-14), does a paid hosted tier operated by the maintainer personally create a conflict that the neutral-org move was meant to avoid?
